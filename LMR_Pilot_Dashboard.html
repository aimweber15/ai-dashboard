Methodology notes

  • Per-installer rate: capacity model, not calendar throughput. Each installer is assumed to work hrs/week ( hrs/month using a 30.44-day month). Divided by the pilot's average total labor time per install ( hrs — install + drive + coordination time, averaged across installs with recorded time; PILOT-041 and PILOT-047 excluded, no time data on file) = installs/installer/month. This is a theoretical-capacity ceiling (full working hours spent installing, no scheduling gaps, no non-install duties).
  • Pilot calendar pace (reference line): the rate the pilot actually delivered — installs ÷ pilot installers ÷ the pilot's real calendar span (, months) = installs/installer/month. Shown alongside the capacity-model rate in the reconciliation panel and completion-time card above — the two are roughly x apart, reflecting scheduling gaps, non-install duties, and other real-world friction the capacity model doesn't account for.
  • Cost: built entirely from the contractor's per-unit rate table (no actual-cost field exists in the pilot data) × the LMR mix set above.
  • Accuracy: PILOT-041 and PILOT-047 are excluded from all success-rate math — their final-visit outcome was never recorded (source flag: "No technician notes row on file"). PILOT-024 is excluded from cost/mix totals only (missing LMR type) but counted normally toward accuracy and the per-installer rate.
  • Realistic / at risk / not realistic: compares projected timeline to the proposed timeline and projected cost to the proposed budget. Realistic = projected ≤ proposed on both. At risk = up to 25% over proposed on the worse of the two. Not realistic = more than 25% over. Thresholds are shown so you can judge the underlying ratios directly, not just the label.
